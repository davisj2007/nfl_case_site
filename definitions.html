Definitions

Every number on the site, first in plain English and then the math version for anyone who wants it. Unless I say otherwise, everything is from the point of view of the team with the ball.

Play value

CASE

How much a play changed the expected final margin for the team with the ball, in points. Zero means the play did exactly what the situation called for.

CASEi = pointsi + f · V(si+1) − V(si), where V(s) is the expected net points from state s to the end of the game and f is +1 if the same team has the ball next, −1 if possession changes. Summed over a game, CASE equals the final margin minus V at kickoff, exactly.

Points now

The part of a play's value that comes from the race to the next score. This is the piece EPA also measures.

pointsi + f · A(si+1) − A(si), where A(s) is the expected points of the next score. The analogue of EPA, derived from the same value function.

The game after

The part of a play's value that comes after the next score - mostly the clock, and how many possessions each team has left.

f · B(si+1) − B(si), where B(s) is the expected net points after the next score. V = A + B, so points now + the game after = CASE, exactly.

CASE Margin

Where the game is expected to end up: the current score plus whatever the situation is still worth. It's the line on each game's chart.

margin + V(s), from the offense's side. The line on each game's chart.

EPA

Expected points added, straight from nflfastR. It's on the site for comparison, since it only looks at the race to the next score.

nflfastR's epa column, unchanged.

Win probability

CASE Win Probability

The chance the team with the ball wins. It comes from CASE's expected final margin, and in the last few minutes it also accounts for timeouts and whether the leader can run out the clock.

z = (margin + V) / max(σ(s), 1), where σ(s) is a fitted model of how much the final margin can still move. In regulation, WP = Φ(z + a(t) + b(t)·z + timeout and kneel-out terms), a probit mapping on t = log(1 + seconds left), fitted on completed seasons with shrinkage so each term is zero wherever the data do not support it. In overtime, WP = Φ(z).

Leverage

How much a play could have swung who wins. In a close fourth quarter it's high; in a blowout it's close to zero.

w = dWP/dz, the slope of the win-probability curve at the play: φ(zmapped) · dzmapped/dz.

Contested CASE

CASE weighted by leverage, so plays with the game on the line count for more than garbage-time snaps.

Per play: Σ w·CASE / Σ w. Total: Σ w·CASE — a weighted sum, not plays × per play, so snaps in a decided game total little.

Teams and players

Produced, allowed and net

What a team's offense produced, what its defense allowed, and the difference between the two. For allowed, lower is better.

Produced sums CASE over the team's offensive plays; allowed sums the same plays grouped by the defense, so lower is better; net = produced − allowed. Available as CASE and as Contested CASE.

Units

Passing, rushing, special teams, penalties, and clock management. Every play lands in exactly one, so the units add up to the team.

An exclusive partition of every play, so the units add up to the team total.

Player views

Everything a player was involved in, split into passing, rushing, and receiving. A completion counts fully for both the passer and the receiver, so players don't add up to their team.

Tabulations, not allocations: a completion counts in full for both the passer and the receiver, so players do not add up to the team.

Win expectancy

How likely a team was to win based on how it actually played in a game, ignoring the final score.

A logistic model of the result on each side's Contested CASE per play in the game, fitted on completed seasons.

Wins against production

How far a team's record is ahead of (or behind) how it's actually played. It looks backward - it isn't a prediction.

Actual wins minus the sum of win expectancy across the team's games. Descriptive, not a forecast.

When the wins were won

A team's wins piling up across game time, added over all of its games. It finishes at the team's record.

Win probability summed across a team's regular-season games at each second of game time; it ends at the team's record.

Games

Excitement

How surprising a game was, play by play. A swing from 1% to 10% counts for way more than one from 41% to 50%, because it was way less expected.

Σ KL(WPi+1 ‖ WPi) in bits: the information divergence from the win probability before each play to the one after it, summed over the game.

Tension

How long a game stayed up for grabs. A 1 is a coin flip the whole way; close to 0 means it was never really in doubt.

The mean binary entropy of the win probability, in bits.

Plays in doubt

The share of a game's plays where either team still had a real shot.

The share of plays with the home team's win probability between 25% and 75%.

Lowest win probability

How close the winner came to losing.

The minimum of the eventual winner's win probability across the game.

Lead changes

How many times the lead flipped on the scoreboard.

Changes in the sign of the scoreboard margin across the game's plays.

Every number carries

As of

When a number was calculated. A week 8 number can change by week 9 - not because the model changed, but because more of the season happened.

Every table is stamped with as_of (season and week) and the model version that scored it. Models are fitted on completed seasons and applied forward.
